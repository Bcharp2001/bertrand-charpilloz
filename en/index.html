The Singer-Songwriter

Singing what you have lived

Born on 20 January 1966 in Saverne, Alsace, to a Swiss father and a mother from Martinique, Bertrand Charpilloz grew up with the accordion and an early love of melody and rhythm. He first built a career as a management consultant.

Two ordeals changed everything. On 20 January 1992, his 26th birthday, his elder sister Dominique died in the Mont Sainte-Odile air crash. On 18 March 1997, a haemorrhagic stroke left him in a coma. He woke up paralysed on one side and unable to speak, and spent three years learning to walk and talk again, partly by singing. He tells the story of this recovery in « Mes liaisons cérébrales » (2013).

From those years he drew a simple philosophy: keep things in perspective, let go, choose love over despair and poetry over chaos. « Rien n'est jamais perdu » (nothing is ever lost), reads the subtitle of his book. His 21 original songs carry that conviction: lyrics without artifice, between puns, portraits of women, places and meditations on time, in the tradition of Serge Gainsbourg, Alain Bashung, Benjamin Biolay and Alain Souchon.

He has devoted himself to music since 2009. With pianist Marie-Françoise Coelho, he now takes these songs on stage for everyone looking for something to hold on to: you can lose the thread, and tie it again.

The Pianist

At the piano: Marie-Françoise Coelho

Trained as a pianist and singer, Marie-Françoise Coelho entered the conservatoire at 6 and won a first prize in piano and music theory at 16. She then turned to theatre, with the Théâtre Jeune Public in Strasbourg and at street-theatre festivals.

Since 1999 she has focused on voice work. As a dubbing and voice-over actress, she lends her voice to documentaries and reports, notably for Arte, to programme idents and to audiobooks, including Virginie Grimaldi's « Le premier jour du reste de ma vie » (Frémeaux & Associés) and the children's book « Garance et le maître des couleurs ».

Both musician and actress, she brings the duo her piano and the ear of a performer of texts. « Mon Altesse Ego » is dedicated to her.

Frequently Asked Questions

The essentials about Bertrand Charpilloz, his repertoire and the recital L'Altesse Ego.

Who is Bertrand Charpilloz?

Bertrand Charpilloz is a French singer-songwriter and lyricist, born on 20 January 1966 in Saverne, Alsace. He presents a repertoire of 21 original songs in French and the piano and voice recital L'Altesse Ego with pianist Marie-Françoise Coelho.

What kind of music does he make?

French literary chanson, between intimate spoken-sung delivery and cabaret blues, carried by a deep baritone voice. His references are Serge Gainsbourg, Alain Bashung, Benjamin Biolay and Alain Souchon.

What philosophy do his songs carry?

A philosophy born of hardship: keep things in perspective, let go, choose love over despair and poetry over chaos. The subtitle of his book sums it up: « Rien n'est jamais perdu. L'important est de ne jamais désespérer. » (Nothing is ever lost. What matters is never to despair.)

Does he write his own songs?

Yes. Bertrand Charpilloz writes the lyrics and music of all 21 songs in his catalogue.

Which songs should I listen to first?

« Sunset Boulevard », « Sur le creux de ma main », « Le Fil Rouge » and « Mon Altesse Ego », each as an arranged demo and a piano and voice demo on this page.

What is the difference between « L'Altesse Ego » and « Mon Altesse Ego »?

« L'Altesse Ego » is the name of the piano and voice recital. « Mon Altesse Ego » is a song from the repertoire, written as a tribute to pianist Marie-Françoise Coelho.

Who is Marie-Françoise Coelho?

Marie-Françoise Coelho is the pianist of the « L'Altesse Ego » recital. Trained as a pianist and singer (first prize in piano and music theory at 16), she has also been a dubbing and voice-over actress since 1999: documentaries, notably for Arte, and audiobooks, including Virginie Grimaldi's « Le premier jour du reste de ma vie ».

How is his music linked to his stroke?

After a haemorrhagic stroke in 1997, he told the story of his recovery in the book « Mes liaisons cérébrales » (2013) and then in the song « Le Fil Rouge ».

What does the recital need?

A piano and voice duo of 75 to 90 minutes, or a 35-minute opening set. A team of 2 to 3 people, a tech rider of one piano and one vocal mic, soundcheck in 45 minutes, for venues of 80 to 500 seats.

How can I book the recital or receive the lyrics?

Through the Booking section of this page: the full booklet of the 21 lyrics (PDF, in French) and the demos are sent on request to publishers, labels, music supervisors and programmers.
